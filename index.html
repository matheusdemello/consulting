01Robotics & automation

A picking system has to do more than recognize an object. It has to decide how to reach it, grasp it, and complete the action.

The obstacle

Thin, transparent bags packed closely together are difficult to separate in sensor data. Object detection alone does not provide the position, orientation, and grasp information a robot needs.

The work

I connected point-cloud segmentation, 6D pose estimation, grasp planning, and robot control in one picking pipeline. Each stage carried the information needed by the next, from identifying an individual object to executing the grasp.

What was demonstrated

The integrated system performed autonomous picks without human intervention during evaluation runs. Pick success was measured on the robot, so the result covered the full perception-to-action pipeline.

For robotics teams working with clutter, difficult materials, and the gap between perception and control.

02Document intelligence

Engineering knowledge loses its value when finding it means opening report after report. The answer also needs a source you can check.

The obstacle

Important findings were spread across report text, scanned pages, diagrams, and handwritten annotations. Exact product identifiers and technical relationships were easy to lose in a text-only search.

The work

I combined OCR and visual-language extraction with retrieval using keywords, semantic similarity, metadata, and relationships between records. The work covered both recovering information from the page and finding the passages relevant to a question.

What changed

Engineers could retrieve earlier findings with links back to their sources. Evaluation covered extraction, retrieval, and answer grounding so failures could be investigated at the stage where they occurred.

For engineering and knowledge teams working with technical reports, scans, tables, and diagrams.

03Geospatial & energy

An aerial image shows where a roof is. Assessing solar potential also needs its geometry, surrounding shadows, and surface conditions.

The obstacle

Roof assessment needed satellite imagery, LiDAR, meshes, and point clouds to agree on the same geometry. Slope, orientation, shadow, and reflectance all mattered to the analysis.

The work

I combined these data sources for 3D roof modeling and solar-potential analysis. The processing workflow covered tiling, denoising, mask generation, coordinate alignment, and the steps before and after model inference.

What changed

The workflow supported per-building investment-return assessment and reduced analysis costs through automation. It made roof geometry and local conditions part of the assessment rather than relying on imagery alone.

For geospatial, energy, and infrastructure teams turning imagery and 3D data into site-level assessments.

04Medical-image R&D

A small anatomical structure can matter to the task even when it occupies very little of the image. Its boundary is often the difficult part.

The obstacle

Small target classes and severe class imbalance make medical-image model development difficult. Ambiguous anatomical boundaries add uncertainty to both annotation and evaluation.

The work

I developed machine-learning components for bone, cartilage, and osteophyte analysis as part of knee-planning software, with attention to the structures and boundaries that are easily missed.

Scope of the work

This case describes image-analysis research and software development. It does not establish clinical performance or patient outcomes.

For medical-software R&D teams developing image-analysis models for small or ambiguous targets.

05Scientific computing & materials

When material behavior depends on many interacting molecules, simulation can connect the microscopic interactions to the patterns that emerge.

The question

How do molecular interactions produce orientational order, spontaneous polarization, and domains in a liquid crystal? Answering it requires a connection between individual molecules and collective behavior.

The work

I used quantum-chemical calculations to study molecular interactions and incorporated those results into classical molecular-dynamics force fields. The simulations examined molecular alignment, polarization, and three-dimensional defect structures.

What the work established

The analysis clarified mechanisms behind phase transitions and domain formation. This was a research contribution supported by simulation and peer-reviewed publication.

For materials and research teams investigating mechanisms through numerical models and simulation.
